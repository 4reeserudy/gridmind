What is being solved

Everything is per-unit on the inverter apparent-power rating. The grid is a Thevenin source behind the line impedance you set; the PCC voltage is the source magnitude plus the voltage rise the inverter itself creates. 

V_pcc  =  Vs + R·P + X·Q                      network coupling
P_ref  =  P0 − (kp/100)·dz(f − 60, db_f)      frequency-watt
Q_ref  =  −(kq/100)·dz(V − Vref, db_v)/0.01   volt-var
Tp·dP  =  P_ref − P     Tq·dQ = Q_ref − Q     open-loop response time
2H·dω  =  Pg + r·ΔP − ΔP_load − D·ω           swing equation
Tg·dPg =  −Pg − Kg·ω                          rest-of-fleet governor
dz(x,db) = x−db (x>db), x+db (x<−db), else 0  deadband

Why the loop can ring

Volt-var control closes a loop through the network: the inverter changes Q, which changes the PCC voltage it is measuring. Loop gain is X · dQ/dV. Raise kq or the line reactance far enough and the response becomes underdamped, then oscillatory — the classic weak-grid volt-var hunting seen on high-penetration feeders. Slowing the response time (larger Tq) damps it; the deadband stops the small-signal hunting around the reference.

Reference settings

IEEE 1547-2018 defaults: frequency deadband 0.036 Hz, 5 % droop (≈33.3 % of rating per Hz), open-loop response time 0.2–10 s, and Category B reactive capability of 44 % of rating. Rule 21 volt-var uses a piecewise curve rather than a single slope; the single slope with deadband here is its linearised equivalent.

Simplifications worth knowing

  • Linearised voltage rise — no full load-flow, no angle dynamics.
  • No current-controller or PLL dynamics; the response-time lag stands in for them.
  • Frequency is system-wide (single bus) — no inter-area modes.
  • No ride-through trip logic, no harmonics, no unbalance.
